# What We Sell

The product menu, the portfolio question, and how to sell technology to people
who don't know what technology can do.

## The portfolio question

"Don't build before payment" was too absolute. The point was to stop building
*product infrastructure*. **Sales collateral is a different thing**, and you
cannot walk into a shop with nothing to show.

**Weak proof:** pretty mockups of imaginary businesses. Anyone can produce
those, and buyers know it.

**Strong proof:** a working demo of the exact thing they would get. Build one
demo site with a live chatbot on it. Then hand the owner your phone and let
them type *"do you service Midlothian?"* and watch it answer.

Worth more than an hour of explaining. Roughly 4-6 hours to build, since every
component already exists.

> **One demo. Not five.** Label it honestly as a demo, never imply it is a
> client. Fabricated proof is the one mistake that is hard to recover from in a
> word-of-mouth market.

### Proof you already have

| Asset | Status |
|---|---|
| Own site, with a working AI assistant | Live |
| The demo site | Live at /demo.html and /demo-es.html |
| Mom's business | First real build, genuinely authentic |
| Founding clients 2 and 3 | Not yet |

The founding-client structure exists to manufacture real proof fast. The
discount buys testimonials, not goodwill.

## The product menu

**"Personalized technical solution" is unsellable.** A non-technical buyer
cannot evaluate it, compare it, or price it. Ambiguity feels like risk, and
risk means no. They need a menu. Personalization is *how you deliver*, not
*what you sell*.

### Tier 1, the front door

**The website.** $800 / $1,200 / $1,800. Lead with this always. It is the one
product they already understand and already have a rough price for.

### Tier 2, where the business actually is

**The monthly plan.** $149/month. Hosting, the AI assistant running, small
changes, a monthly check-in.

**Attach it to every single deal from day one.**

### Tier 3, cookie-cutter add-ons

Built once, configured per client, sold repeatedly.

| Add-on | Setup | Monthly | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missed-call text-back | | +$49 | Same pain, same pitch. **Not built yet** |
| Google Business Profile setup + monthly posts | $200 | +$49 | Most local businesses have this half-configured |
| Review request automation | | +$39 | **Not built yet** |
| Booking / scheduling integration | $150 | | One-time, high perceived value |
| Monthly performance report | | +$29 | Or include it, to make the base plan feel richer |

A client on the base plan plus two add-ons is ~$250/month. Ten of those is
$30k a year, before a single new build.

> [!warning] Do not sell what is not built
> Missed-call text-back and review-request automation appear in this menu as
> *planned*. Neither exists. Nothing here is quoted to a prospect until it is
> built and running on a live reference client.

### Tier 4, custom work

**Not a product.** Quoted hourly or per project, only after a relationship
exists. Never lead with it. "I can build whatever you need" reads as *I don't
know what I do*.

## Selling to people who don't know what's possible

Buyers do not know what technology can do for them, so they cannot ask for it.
**Stop selling capability and start selling a problem they already complain
about.** They cannot evaluate "AI automation." They can absolutely evaluate
*"you missed six calls last week."*

### 1. Ask about their day, not their technology

> "What happens when you're busy and the phone rings?"
> "How do people usually find you?"
> "What's a customer worth to you, roughly?"

They will describe the problem themselves. Far stronger than you asserting it,
and it tells you which product to offer.

### 2. Show, don't describe

Hand them the phone. Let them break it. Nothing you say competes with them
watching it work.

### 3. Count in their units

Not features, **jobs**.

> "You said a job's worth about four grand. If this catches one call a year
> that would've gone to voicemail, it's paid for itself twice over."

### 4. Never say AI, automation, or dashboard first

| Don't say | Say |
|---|---|
| AI chatbot | Answers your phone when you can't |
| Automation | Texts them back so they don't call someone else |
| Dashboard | Tells you what's actually bringing you work |
| Responsive design | Works on a phone, because that's where they'll see it |
| SEO | Shows up when someone searches for your trade near them |

## The reframe that matters most

**They don't need to know what's possible. You do.**

Translating a complaint into a working solution *is* the service. They are not
buying software, they are buying not having to think about this.

This is also why the discovery conversation is not preparation for the sale.
**It is the sale.** Asking better questions than anyone else has asked them
makes you the expert without saying one technical word.
