# Proposal Template

Sent one to two days after a good conversation. **Keep it to one page.** A long
proposal reads as effort spent on paperwork rather than on them.

Replace everything in `[brackets]`.

## The template

> # Proposal for [Business Name]
> **Prepared for [Owner Name], [Date]**
>
> ## What you told me
>
> [Restate THEIR problem in THEIR words. This is the most important paragraph
> in the document.]
>
> *Example: You said you're missing five or six calls a week when you're out on
> jobs, and that a typical install is worth around $4,000. You've got a website
> your nephew built a few years back, but nothing catches the people who
> message after hours.*
>
> ## What I'd build
>
> **[Package Name], $[Price]**
>
> - [Page count] pages, built to work properly on a phone, which is where most
>   of your customers will see it
> - [The AI assistant that answers questions and takes messages day and night]
> - [Set up on Google so you show up when someone searches for a [category]
>   near [their area]]
> - [Anything specific they mentioned]
>
> **Timeline:** 1-2 weeks from the day I get your completed questionnaire back.
>
> ## What happens after
>
> **$149/month.** Hosting, the assistant running, changes whenever you need
> them, and a monthly check-in so you can see what's coming in.
>
> First [1/3] month[s] included. Cancel any time.
>
> ## What it's worth
>
> [Do the arithmetic using THEIR number.]
>
> *Example: You said a typical install runs about $4,000. If this catches one
> job a year that would otherwise have gone to voicemail, it's paid for itself
> twice over.*
>
> ## What I need from you
>
> 1. Say yes and I'll send a short agreement
> 2. [50% deposit / $[X] deposit] to get started
> 3. A questionnaire about your business, takes about 15 minutes, and I write
>    everything from your answers
>
> **Two rounds of changes are included.** After that it's hourly, so you always
> know where you stand.
>
> You'll own your domain. I handle the hosting.
>
> **Kevin Chavez**, KC IT Solutions
> (540) 327-2698, KC.ITSolutions@outlook.com
> Happy to talk it through: cal.com/kcitsolution

## Why it's written this way

- **"What you told me" comes first.** It proves you listened and makes the rest
  feel bespoke rather than generic. If you can't fill this section, the
  discovery conversation wasn't good enough. Go back and have another one.
- **Their number, their maths.** "One job pays for it twice over" only works
  when *they* supplied the job value.
- **The monthly plan is included, not bolted on later.**
- **Scope limits appear as reassurance**, not restriction: "so you always know
  where you stand."
- **One page.** If it runs to three, cut.
