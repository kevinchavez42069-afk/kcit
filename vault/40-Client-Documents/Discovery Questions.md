# Discovery Questions

The conversation that happens before any quote. **This is not preparation for
the sale, it is the sale.** Asking better questions than anyone else has asked
them is what makes you the expert, without saying one technical word.

**Do not bring a laptop. Bring a notepad.** Writing down their answers signals
you're listening.

> [!note] Numbering
> Notion restarted the count in each section, which broke the callouts that
> reference "question 8" and "question 14". Renumbered continuously here so the
> references work.

## Their business first

1. How long have you been doing this?
2. What kind of work do you do most of, and what's the work you actually
   *want* more of?
3. Roughly what's a typical job worth to you?
4. How many people work here?

> **Question 3 matters most.** Every number later in the conversation gets
> compared against it. If a job is worth $4,000, then $1,800 is not expensive,
> but you can only make that argument if they said the number, not you.

## How customers find them

5. When someone needs you, how do they usually find you?
6. Where does most of your work come from right now: referrals, Google,
   Facebook, repeat customers?
7. Do you get much from Google, or is it mostly word of mouth?

## The pain, the heart of it

8. **What happens when someone calls and you're busy on a job?**
9. Do people message you on Facebook or your website? What happens to those?
10. Roughly how many calls a week do you think you miss?
11. Has a customer ever told you they'd tried to reach you and gave up?

> **Question 8 is the one.** Ask it and then **stop talking.** Let the silence
> do the work. Most owners have never been asked this and will talk themselves
> into the problem far more convincingly than you could.

## What they have now

12. Do you have a website? Who built it?
13. What did it cost, and are you happy with it?
14. Do you use anything for scheduling or invoicing?
15. Are you getting Google reviews? Do you ask for them?

> **Question 14** tells you whether you're complementary or competing with
> something they already pay for. **Question 15** often uncovers an easier,
> cheaper first sale than a website.

## Closing the conversation

16. If I could set it up so nothing slips through after hours, is that worth a
    conversation?
17. Who else would be part of that decision?
18. What's the best number to reach you on?

> **Question 17** saves you from a great meeting with someone who can't say yes.

## What you are actually listening for

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| They name a number of missed calls | Strong lead. The pain is already quantified in their head |
| "My nephew built it" | Don't criticise it. Ask if it answers customers at night |
| High job value, low web presence | Best possible fit |
| "We're too busy for more work" | Real answer sometimes. Move on, stay friendly, follow up in six months |
| They ask what it costs | Buying signal. Give the number plainly, don't dodge |

## What not to do

- **Don't pitch during discovery.** You're gathering, not selling.
- **Don't quote on the spot** unless they push. "Let me put something together
  for you" is stronger, and gives you a reason to follow up.
- **Don't say AI, automation, dashboard, or SEO.** Not once.
- **Don't fill silences.** The pause after question 8 is the most valuable ten
  seconds of the conversation.
