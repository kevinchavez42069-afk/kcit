# Serving Hispanic-Owned Businesses

Bilingual capability, and how to talk about it.

> [!warning] Live override
> The original called this "the clearest competitive advantage" and treated it
> as the moat. **Demoted.** It is one strong differentiator among several. Not
> the lead pitch, not a targeting filter, and not the only focus. Surface it
> when a prospect would actually benefit.

## The framing rule

Get this wrong and nothing else matters.

These owners are **not behind on technology. They are underserved by it.**
Someone running a busy restaurant on thin margins has plenty of operational
sophistication. What they lack is a vendor who explains options in their
language without condescending.

Copy that reads as *"let me bring you up to speed"* fails. Copy that reads as
*"finally, someone who speaks your language and won't talk down to you"* lands.

## Why the product is genuinely better here

A business serving both English and Spanish speaking customers has a real
staffing problem: whoever answers the phone needs both languages. An AI
assistant solves that natively. It was not a feature that had to be built, it
had to stop being suppressed. The old prompt said "Plain English," which pushed
the bot to answer a Spanish speaker in English.

## Cautions

**Word of mouth is far stronger in tight business communities.** One delighted
client can produce five referrals. A bad first job travels just as fast.

**Do not assume language preference.** Many owners are bilingual or
English-dominant. Offer it, do not presume it.

**The LLC matters more here, not less.** Communities burned by vendors place
real weight on a business being properly registered.
