# The Plan

How KC IT Solutions goes from zero clients to a real business. Written
2026-08-31. A critical plan, not an encouraging one.

> [!note] Superseded in part
> This was written when the plan was to stay narrow. Target market is now
> **any business**, not just trades. Treat the niche argument below as the
> reasoning of the time, and the economics as still useful.

## Three uncomfortable truths

### 1. Nobody has been asked to buy anything yet

The website is live. The AI assistant works. The infrastructure is secured.
Zero customers have been spoken to.

### 2. The chatbot is already ahead of demand

Multi-tenant, origin-validated, rate-limited, cost-attributed, for zero paying
clients. Justified as a demo and differentiator. Not justified as ongoing
investment.

### 3. The capacity ceiling is lower than it looks

With a day job, realistic capacity is 10-15 hours a week.

| Package | Realistic build hours | Elapsed at 12 hrs/wk |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | 15-20 | ~1.5 weeks |
| Standard | 25-30 | ~2.5 weeks |
| Premium | 30-40 | ~3 weeks |

Roughly **15-20 builds a year**, and only if every hour goes to delivery.

**Consequence:** one-time builds cannot get this to meaningful income.
Recurring revenue is the only version that works part-time.

## Phase 0: Foundation

| # | Action | Why it cannot wait |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Turn on MFA for the AWS root account | Root with no MFA is how people lose an account. Done 2026-09-01 |
| 2 | Form a Virginia LLC (~$100 + $50/yr) | No separation between business and personal liability |
| 3 | Open a business bank account | Mixing funds undermines the LLC protection |
| 4 | Create the Google Business Profile | Largest local-search factor. Verification takes days |
| 5 | Decide the six open terms | Undefined scope turns a $1,200 build into three months of unpaid work |

## Phase 1: Twenty conversations

The goal is NOT to sell. Ask, don't pitch:

- What happens when someone calls and you're busy?
- How do people find you now?
- What did your website cost, and are you happy with it?
- Roughly what's a customer worth to you?

**What is actually being tested:** whether missed contact is a pain owners
recognise and can quantify.

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 8+ of 20 volunteer it as a real problem | Thesis confirmed, proceed |
| 3-7 do | Weak. Re-examine before building more |
| Under 3 | The pitch is wrong. Change it. Do not proceed on hope |

## Phase 2: Three founding clients

1. **One genuine freebie**, a family or friend's business. Becomes the first
   portfolio piece.
2. **Two founding clients** at $500 with a $100-200 deposit, plus $99/month.

Sell the monthly plan from day one. Retrofitting it later is far harder.

Before work starts, in writing: testimonial, permission to use photos,
permission to be named, willingness to take a reference call.

### Hard rules

- Two discounted slots. Then stop.
- Scope in writing before starting.
- Photograph and document everything. You are manufacturing proof.

## Phase 3: Proof, then full price

- Three case studies with real numbers
- Add the headshot
- **Charge full price from client four onward.** No exceptions
- Build the onboarding automation now, when there is real repetition
- Move lead delivery to AWS SES

## Phase 4: One channel, worked hard

Pick the single channel that actually produced clients and do more of it. Do
not run six channels badly. Target 10-15 clients on monthly plans by month six.

## Phase 5: The decision

| Outcome by month 6 | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 12+ clients, $1,500+/mo recurring | It works | Plan the transition off the day job |
| 5-11 clients, growing | Real but slow | Continue part-time |
| Under 5 after genuine effort | Market not responding | Stop, or change something fundamental |

### Kill criteria

Stop or pivot if, after six months of genuine effort: fewer than 5 paying
clients; nobody renews past month three; consistently working past midnight
and dreading it.

## What will actually kill this

| Risk | Defence |
|---|---|
| Burnout | Cap at 15 hrs/week. Say no to the fourth simultaneous client |
| One bad client | Scope in writing. Revisions numbered. Be willing to walk away |
| Underpricing | Full price from client four. Never discount to win an argument |
| Discount creep | Two slots. Then stop |
| No recurring revenue | Sell the monthly plan on every deal from day one |

## The numbers to watch

Four numbers, weekly: conversations had, proposals sent, clients on monthly
plans, hours worked. Revenue is a lagging indicator. Conversations predict it.
