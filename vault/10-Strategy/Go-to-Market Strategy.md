# Go-to-Market Strategy

How KC IT Solutions gets its first clients.

> [!warning] Live override
> The niche section below argues for HVAC, plumbing, and electrical only.
> **That no longer applies.** Target market is now any business, any type.
> The economic reasoning is still the useful part: work out what one lost
> customer is worth for the business in front of you, then price from that.

## The opener

Lead with a question, not a statement:

> When someone messages your business at 9pm on a Saturday, does anything catch
> that, or does it sit till Monday?

Then:

> I build sites and AI assistants for local shops so that doesn't happen. Most
> of what I do pays for itself off one job you'd have otherwise lost.

**Do not lead with "I build websites."** That is a commodity and it loses to
Wix on price. Lead with the lost job.

## Why the original niche was chosen

Retained as reasoning, not as a restriction:

- A single missed call can be a $3,000-8,000 job, so $1,800 is trivially
  justified
- The pain is urgent and binary, not aesthetic
- Owners are physically findable, which matters when in-person is the best
  channel
- Digitally underserved

**Apply the same test to any category.** Where the number is smaller, the
argument shifts to repeat bookings and no-shows, and the honest recommendation
is a cheaper package.

## The first three clients

1. **One genuine freebie**, a family or friend's business
2. **Two founding-client slots** at a stated discount, e.g. $500 instead of $800

Framed as limited founding-client pricing, **not free work**. Get in writing
before starting: testimonial, permission to use photos, permission to name
them, and a reference call if asked.

## Outreach, ranked

| # | Channel | Cost | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | In-person visits | Gas | 5-8 hrs/wk | Highest conversion at zero reputation |
| 2 | Warm network asks | $0 | ~1 hr | Trust is inherited |
| 3 | Referral partners (bookkeepers, insurance agents) | $0 | 1-2 hrs | Slow, compounds |
| 4 | Google Business Profile / local SEO | $0 | Passive | Infrastructure, not a lead channel |
| 5 | Chamber / BNI groups | $400-1,000/yr | 3-6 mo runway | Mid-term |
| 6 | Organic Instagram / Facebook | $0 | Ongoing | Supports trust after a meeting, doesn't create one |

### Deliberately cut

- **Paid ads.** Nothing to advertise until there are case studies
- **Cold email, cold SMS, scraped lists, auto-dialing.** CAN-SPAM and TCPA
  exposure, and it poisons reputation in a word-of-mouth market

## The QR leave-behind

The card carries a QR code pointing at the live chat widget. A prospect scans
it and a working AI assistant answers questions about the business. That is the
product demonstrating itself.

## Scripts

### Walking in, 15 seconds

> "Hey, I'm Kevin. I build websites and AI chat assistants for local shops so
> you stop losing jobs to missed calls or after-hours messages. Not selling
> anything today, just wanted to introduce myself. Who normally handles your
> website?"

"Not selling anything today" removes the reason to brush you off, and ending on
a question about *them* keeps the conversation open.

### Follow-up text, a few days later

> "Hey [name], it's Kevin. Stopped by [business] last week. No pressure, but if
> you want, I can show you a 2-minute example of what I mean about the
> after-hours thing."

> [!note] Rewritten during migration
> The original used an em dash after "Kevin". Replaced with a period per
> [[voice]]. The vault should not ship an example that breaks its own rules.

### Objections

**"I already have a website"**

> "Totally fine. Quick check: if someone messages you on Facebook at 9pm
> wanting a quote, does anything catch that, or does it sit til morning?
> That's usually the actual gap, not the site itself."

**"Can't afford it"**

> "Fair. For context, most of what I build pays for itself off one job you'd
> have otherwise missed. I can also do it in two payments. No pressure either
> way, here's my card."

**"My nephew does that"**

> "That's great, honestly. Has he set it up so it answers customers
> automatically, even at night? If so you're already ahead of most people I
> talk to."

Never criticise the nephew. Raise the bar instead.

**"I'm too busy"**

> "That's the whole point. I set it up, you don't touch it, you just get the
> leads. One question: is missed contact actually costing you customers, or
> not really?"

**The pattern:** never defend the product, never argue with the objection.
Redirect to a specific question they have to actually think about. The question
does the selling.
