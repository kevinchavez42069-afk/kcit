## Current web presence

No dedicated website found. Checked Birdeye, Yelp, Yellow Pages, Groupon,
Wellness.com, and their Facebook page directly, none show a business-owned
domain. Booking runs entirely through phone calls to the salon. Facebook and
Instagram exist but function as a photo feed, not a booking or contact
channel.

## Lead-capture gap

This is not a guess. A review on their Birdeye profile reads: "Please call
804-378-0872 that is actually our main line. If you call the 0888 number it
goes straight to voicemail." The 0888 number is the one that surfaces in
Yellow Pages and other directory listings when you search for this business.
That means a real, findable fraction of the people trying to reach this salon
by the number the internet hands them are hitting voicemail, not a person, and
there's no indication anyone calls them back from it.

On top of that: closed Saturday and Sunday, with phone as the only contact
method. Anyone who thinks of booking over the weekend, which is a normal time
to plan a haircut, has no way to reach them until Monday.

## What one lost customer is worth

Blonde/balayage color work runs meaningfully above a basic cut, typically
$100-200 per visit at a specialty salon, and colored hair needs upkeep every
6-8 weeks. That's 6-8 visits a year from a retained client, roughly
$700-1,200/year, and colorist relationships tend to run for years once
trust is built, so a real client is worth several thousand dollars over time.
Salon work is also visibly referral-driven: a client complimented on fresh
color at work or a wedding is a walking advertisement for the person who did
it.

**Estimate:** $2,000-4,000+ lifetime value per retained color client, plus
referral effects that are hard to price but real in this business.

## Recommended package

**Premium.** This isn't a hypothetical missed-call argument, it's a
documented one: a specific, currently-listed phone number sends real callers
to voicemail with no follow-up. A chatbot on an owned site removes the
dependency on directory accuracy entirely and gives after-hours and weekend
visitors a way to actually get an answer or leave a request instead of
hitting a dead end. Given the repeat frequency and per-visit value here,
recovering even one client a quarter pays for Premium well inside a year.
